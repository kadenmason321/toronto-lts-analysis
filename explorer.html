Can Toronto be crossed entirely on low-stress streets?
No.
Toronto's low-stress cycling network isn't one connected system -- it's fragmented into over 1,400 disconnected pieces. The largest single piece holds only 14% of the city's low-stress streets. A cyclist who only feels safe on quiet, protected routes is confined to a small pocket of the city, regardless of where they start.
A quick guide before you dive in
Level of Traffic Stress (LTS) Every street is scored 1-4 based on how comfortable an average adult would feel biking on it -- using facility type, speed, lane count, and parking presence. LTS 1 means you'd feel fine teaching a child to ride there. LTS 4 means only confident, experienced cyclists are comfortable.
Network Connectivity (Fragments) Low-stress streets don't automatically connect to each other. This layer groups streets into "fragments" -- separate, disconnected low-stress networks, each shown in a different color. A cyclist confined to low-stress streets can only travel within a single color, not across colors.
How to explore Hover or tap any street for its details. Use the dropdown (top right) to jump to a different city. Toggle layers on and off in the panel below it -- data only loads when you turn a layer on. Six cities are included: Toronto, Vancouver, Corvallis, Seattle, Amsterdam, and New York City.
